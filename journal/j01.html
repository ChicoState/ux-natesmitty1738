Why Does My Phone Always Forget I Want Speaker?

April 1, 2026  ·  Nate Smith

The Interaction

I get calls all the time from family and friends. Almost every time, the same thing happens. I answer and the audio is already going somewhere I didn't pick. My AirPods, my MacBook, whatever happened to be connected last. The other person starts talking and I can't hear them. I have to say "hold on, one second" before we even get started, just to switch the audio output. Every call.

The situation that gets me most: my AirPods are sitting on the desk, still connected from earlier. My phone rings, iOS sees them connected, and routes the call there. I want it on speaker so I can move around. I've made that exact switch more times than I can count and the phone has no memory of it.

The Button I Shouldn't Have to Press

The in-call screen has an "audio" button that opens a small dropdown with all your output options. A checkmark shows which one is active. On the left below, you can see AirPods were auto-selected. On the right, I've already switched to iPhone.

The core problem is a broken mental model, which is the expectation you build in your head for how something should work. My phone learns my schedule, suggests apps by location, and autocorrects my typing. So I expect it to notice I switch audio outputs on every call. It doesn't. It just picks whatever Bluetooth device connected most recently.

That's a default behavior issue. A default is what a system does when it hasn't been told otherwise. The iOS default is to route calls to the most recently connected Bluetooth device. That rule makes sense in some situations, but it completely ignores any pattern of behavior I've built up.

Steve Krug writes in Don't Make Me Think that users tend to satisfice, meaning they pick the first good-enough option rather than hunting for a perfect one. That's exactly what I do. I switch to speaker and move on. The problem is that even if I wanted a real fix, there isn't one. iOS has no setting for a default call audio output.

There's also a feedback problem. Feedback is how a system tells you what it's doing. There's no warning before I answer showing which output will be active. I only find out once the call connects and I can't hear anything.

The Pattern It Never Learned

After months of this, I started noticing that my iPhone learns plenty of other things. Focus modes switch automatically by time of day. Siri suggests apps before I open them. The capability exists. It just isn't applied here.

Krug also talks about how users muddle through, meaning they figure things out by trial and error rather than reading instructions or digging into settings. That's the whole story of this interaction. I never went looking for a fix. I just kept correcting it each time and moved on.

What Works

  • The audio menu is easy to find and the options are clearly labeled
  • Switching only takes a few taps once you know where to look

How It Could Be Better

The fix seems obvious. Let users set a default audio output for calls, or have the phone learn from repeated behavior. iOS already tracks location and usage patterns for other features. Applying that same logic here would solve the problem without any new technology.
